Where does

Start with the home.

Households account for more food waste than food service and retail combined in UNEP’s global estimates for these three sectors.

WORLDWIDE · ESTIMATES FOR 2022 · REPORT PUBLISHED 2024

01 / UNDERSTAND THE DATA

Three sectors.
One shared problem.

UNEP estimates a total of 1,052 million tonnes of food waste across households, food service and retail in 2022. This includes both edible and inedible parts.

THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS

Food waste is not
all edible food.

The definition includes inedible parts such as bones and peelings. These totals cannot tell us how much of the waste could have been eaten.

02 / READ IT CORRECTLY

What the numbers
can — and cannot — say.

  • Place and time: global estimates for 2022. They are not Swedish figures or measurements for the current year.
  • Scope: households, food service and retail. Agriculture and earlier supply-chain losses are outside this comparison.
  • Uncertainty: data availability and measurement quality vary. UNEP uses studies and extrapolation to estimate global totals; these are not an exact count of every discarded item.
  • Calculation: 631 + 290 + 131 = 1,052 million tonnes. Dividing each sector by 1,052 and rounding to whole percentages gives 60%, 28% and 12%.
  • Interpretation: sector totals describe where waste occurs. They do not establish individual blame, causes or the impact of a particular solution.

Method reference: UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024 — methodological appendix.

03 / CONTENT PLAN

Make the finding
worth sharing.

Objective: help readers understand where food waste occurs and what is included in the figures, then invite them to explore the evidence.

Audience: young adults interested in food and sustainability, plus journalists covering consumer issues, climate and everyday life.

Core message: households dominate the measured three-sector total, but “food waste” includes more than edible leftovers.

Editorial angle: “Households dominate food waste — but how much could actually be eaten?” This is an explanatory story based on existing research, not a newly discovered result.
